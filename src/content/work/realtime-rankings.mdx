## What was at stake

The feature shows creators the top-performing live sessions and products each hour, so they can learn from what's working. The original design read the serving database on every request, scanning across millions of rows, and it couldn't meet its latency budget. It had stalled, and the work in progress was tuning the query.

## Diagnosis

The query wasn't the constraint. The access pattern was. No amount of tuning makes a full scan on every page load fast, because the expensive work was happening at the worst possible moment: while a person waited.

<Callout label="The call">
  Stop computing at read time. Do the heavy work once, as events arrive, and make the page read a precomputed answer.
</Callout>

## What I built

Flink streaming jobs over the live-commerce event stream, 1.5B+ records and 294 GB at parallelism 100, producing hourly revenue and traffic rankings. The results are published through a message queue into a serving store indexed for ranking, so what used to be a scan across millions of rows became a single lookup.

The shape of it, as an illustrative pattern rather than production code:

```sql
-- Flink SQL: hourly rankings computed as events arrive
insert into hourly_rankings
select session_id,
       window_start,
       sum(order_amount) as revenue,
       count(*)          as interactions
from table(
  tumble(table live_events,
         descriptor(event_time),
         interval '1' hour))
group by session_id, window_start, window_end;
```

Privacy was part of the design, not an afterthought: revenue is shown as ranges, and participation is opt-in.

## Result

Rankings return instantly, and query time and compute cost dropped by orders of magnitude. The feature launched for A/B testing.
