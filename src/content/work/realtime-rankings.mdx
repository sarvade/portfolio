## What was at stake

The feature shows creators the top-performing live sessions and products each hour, so they can learn from what is working right now. The original design read the serving database on every request, scanning across millions of rows, and it couldn't meet its latency budget. The feature had stalled, and the effort in flight was tuning the query.

## Diagnosis

Before writing any code, I drew the hour-by-hour data flow on paper (events, windowed aggregation, ranking messages, serving store) and walked it through with the infrastructure owner to check my model against reality. That is where the real constraint showed up.

The query wasn't the problem. The access pattern was. No amount of tuning makes a full scan on every page load fast, because the expensive work was happening at the worst possible moment: while a person waited for it.

<Callout label="The call">
  Stop computing at read time. Do the heavy work once, as events arrive, and make the page read a precomputed answer.
</Callout>

Flink streaming jobs over the live-commerce event stream, 1.5B+ records and 294 GB at parallelism 100, producing hourly revenue and traffic rankings. Results are published through a message queue into a serving store indexed for ranking, so the cost of a page load no longer grows with the number of live sessions.

The shape of it, as an illustrative pattern rather than production code:

```sql
-- Flink SQL: hourly aggregates that feed the rankings, computed as events arrive
insert into hourly_rankings
select session_id,
       window_start,
       sum(order_amount) as revenue,
       count(*)          as interactions
from table(
  tumble(table live_events,
         descriptor(event_time),
         interval '1' hour))
group by session_id, window_start, window_end;
```

## Privacy as a pipeline property

The product shows revenue as ranges rather than exact figures, and participation is opt-in, so creators can benchmark against each other without anyone's earnings being exposed to a peer. I built the pipeline to those constraints from the start, so the privacy property is structural rather than a display-layer promise.

## Result

Rankings return instantly, query time and compute cost dropped by orders of magnitude, and the feature launched for A/B testing. The stalled design was never going to get there with tuning; the redesign of where the work happens did.

## What broke, and what changed

The original design broke on its access pattern: it scanned millions of rows at read time, on every request, so cost grew with the number of live sessions and the page missed its latency budget. Precomputing upstream turned that scan into a lookup.

## What I’d do differently now

- Wire the A/B readout into the pipeline from the first release, so the outcome is a measured lift rather than a launch.
