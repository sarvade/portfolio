## What was at stake

Creators were seeing videos in their list with zero revenue and zero views, and leadership wanted to know why. The story everyone had already settled on was a broken filter for a new content type. Several people were treating it as a defect with a fix to find.

If that story was wrong, the fix would ship, change nothing, and the real problem would stay buried under a closed ticket.

## Eliminate layer by layer

I worked it layer by layer, from the number the creator sees backwards, looking for the layer where it changes.

A fully populated 46-million-row partition ruled out staleness, so any gap had to be item-specific. That killed the filter theory.

## Close what isn't a bug, with receipts

For the three items behind the escalation, I read the realtime Flink job instead of assuming. The flag is a raw passthrough from an upstream event stream another team owns, defaulted to zero when the event hasn't carried a value yet. That is a plausible false negative, so I waited 24 hours to rule out lag, then went to the ground-truth dimension table. All three genuinely were not the content type the ticket assumed. I closed that thread as not-a-bug, with the full evidence chain attached.

<Callout label="The call">
  Closing a ticket without a fix only works with evidence. The full chain went into the thread, and the investigation moved to where the data actually disagreed.
</Callout>

## Then find the one that is

I scaled the check from 3 items to all 236 that had been flagged, in both pipelines.

That isolated a real gap to the realtime pipeline, and specifically to my own realtime transform job rather than the upstream team's stream. The check itself is simple, which is why it is worth running before anyone writes a fix. As an illustrative pattern rather than production code:

```sql
-- Same items, both pipelines, one expected value
select count(*) as items,
       sum(case when coalesce(o.flag, -1) <> f.expected
                then 1 else 0 end) as offline_wrong,
       sum(case when coalesce(r.flag, -1) <> f.expected
                then 1 else 0 end) as realtime_wrong
from      flagged_items f
left join offline_daily o
       on o.item_id = f.item_id and o.dt = '{{ ds }}'
left join realtime_view r
       on r.item_id = f.item_id;
```

One open question could still reframe all of it: did the 236 items come from the live product page, or from a separate dashboard? If it was the dashboard, the investigation was scoped to the wrong surface, and the answer changes what "fixed" even means. It stays on the list until it is answered.

## Where it stands

The team stopped chasing a filter fix that would have changed nothing. The real gap is scoped to one job, and I set up a working group with the realtime, warehouse and product owners to close it. Once it closes, the next number here is the post-fix mismatch rate.
