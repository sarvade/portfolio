## What was at stake

On-call tickets for two products kept arriving in the same shape: "these two numbers don't match." The knowledge to triage them lived in my head, so the quality of triage depended on whether I was awake, on a team spread across three timezones.

## What I built

Three reusable AI assistant skills, each encoding how our stack actually behaves, when to escalate and to whom, and message templates for two audiences: a short engineer-to-engineer version and a plain-language version for product managers.

<Callout label="The design choice that matters">
  Each skill forces one distinction before anything else: is this a data bug, or correct data that needs a product decision? Most of on-call is telling those two apart.
</Callout>

A real example: a product showing up on a video it was never bound to looked like a join defect. It was indirect affiliate tracking working as designed. That is a product call, not an engineering fix, and a triage flow that can't tell the difference sends engineers chasing bugs that aren't there.

They grow as the team learns. When I helped a colleague past an OLAP scan limit, the [diagnosis pattern](../olap-api-cost/) went into the debugging skill so the next person doesn't need either of us.

## Result

Ticket handling became consistent regardless of who picks it up, and new teammates ramp without shadowing me. The measure I care about: the things I wrote get used in threads I'm not in.

## What I’d do differently now

- Track how often each skill resolves a ticket without escalation, so it’s clear which ones earn their keep and which need work.
